# Tracks & Sounds

Pixelboop is organized into four distinct musical tracks. Each track occupies a horizontal section of the 44x24 grid.

## The Four Tracks

1.  **Melody**: The lead voice.
2.  **Chords**: The foundation of your sound.
3.  **Bass**: The low-end energy.
4.  **Rhythm**: The rhythmic pulse (drums).

## Interactive Track Explorer

Each track is listed under the picture below, numbered, with the rows it covers. Tap an entry, or reach it with Tab and the arrow keys, to light that band up in the grid.

## Row 22 — Indicator Lane

Row 22 sits between the Rhythm track (rows 18-21) and the bottom control row (row 23). It is the **Indicator Lane**: a single-row strip that gives you a live overview of your song's structure.

**Tap any step column on row 22** (columns 4 to 35) to switch between two views:

- **Track Colors** (default): each step column lights up with the spectral gradient color of whichever track has a note at that step. This gives you a quick snapshot of pattern density across all tracks.
- **Key Automation**: each step column shows the root-note color recorded at that position. Use this to see how your key changes are distributed across the 32-step timeline.

Pressing and holding a step for about 500 ms means different things in the two views. In Track Colors nothing lands under your finger, and the view still flips when you lift off, which is why a long press feels like it toggles. In Key Automation a hold paints a key marker at that step instead of switching views, so use a quick tap to get back to Track Colors.

## Toggling Steps

Simply tap a pixel to toggle it on or off. The color of the pixel reflects the track it belongs to.
- **Blue-Purple** (`#4D2EF2`): Melody
- **Cyan-Green** (`#00E68C`): Chords
- **Golden Yellow** (`#F2E600`): Bass
- **Red-Orange** (`#FF3000`): Rhythm (Drums)

## Interval Modes

Column 3 of each pitch track shows that track's current scale notes — and holding it lets you cycle the interval mode Melody, Chords, and Bass use. See [Interval Modes](IntervalModes.mdx) for the full gesture and the 8 available modes.
