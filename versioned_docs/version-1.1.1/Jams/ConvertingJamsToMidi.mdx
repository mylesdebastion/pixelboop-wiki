# Converting Jams to MIDI

Any shared Pixelboop jam can be downloaded as a standard **MIDI file** (`.mid`) and opened in any DAW: GarageBand, Logic, Ableton, FL Studio, and the rest. This turns a Pixelboop loop into a starting point you can arrange, re-voice, or layer anywhere.

:::note Depends on saving and sharing your jam
Converting **your own** jam to MIDI builds on two other features. Your jam is first **[saved](./SavingAndOpeningJams)** in the app, then **[shared to the Community gallery](./CommunityJams)** (a TestFlight feature). Once it is in the gallery, you (or anyone) can download it as MIDI. The steps below work for any jam already in the gallery.
:::

## How to download a jam as MIDI

1. Open a shared jam's page. Browse the gallery at **[pixelboop.com/jams](https://pixelboop.com/jams)** and tap any jam, or use a `pixelboop.com/j/<id>` link someone sent you.
2. On the jam page, tap **🎹 Download MIDI**.
3. Open the downloaded `.mid` file in your DAW or any MIDI player.

## What you get

The MIDI file mirrors the jam faithfully:

- **Four tracks**: melody, chords, bass, and drums.
- Melody, chords, and bass each play on their own MIDI channel; **drums are on channel 10** (General MIDI), so they map to a standard drum kit.
- The jam's **tempo, key, and timing** are preserved.
- Each track loads with a sensible synth voice, so it sounds reasonable the moment you open it.

## Sharing your own jam for export

To convert a jam **you** made, first **[save it](./SavingAndOpeningJams)**, then publish it with **[Community Jams](./CommunityJams)** (currently in TestFlight). Once published, your jam gets its own page with the **Download MIDI** button, just like any other. See those two guides for the full steps.
